# CAIOS Skill: Turn Your Best Book Into Something Your AI Actually Knows

We all have that one book, or folder of frameworks, we swear by and then forget by chapter seven. `Leutenegger/book-to-skill` fixes the forgetting. It distills a technical book, a document folder, or a pile of sources into a structured skill your AI loads on demand.

## When to use it
You have a methodology you rely on, a dense industry book, or a stack of your own SOPs, and you want your AI answering from the real content instead of guessing. Reach for it when "I'll just paste the PDF into the chat" keeps giving you vague, half-hallucinated answers.

## Why it's helpful
Dumping a whole book into a prompt is expensive and unreliable. This distills the source into mental models, per-chapter files, a glossary, and a decision cheatsheet, then your AI pulls only the chapter it needs. The README reports 24 to 51 times fewer tokens than stuffing the whole book into context, measured on real books. It keeps the structure, so answers come from the text, not a paraphrase.

## How to make it your own
- Point it at your own material, not just a book. It takes a file, a folder, or a glob, so your SOP library or a client playbook works too.
- It handles PDF, EPUB, DOCX, Markdown, HTML, RTF, and MOBI, so most of what you own is covered.
- Setup is technical: a Python command-line tool (`pip install`, then a few `book-to-skill` commands). If you don't touch a terminal, hand it to someone who does. The output is the reusable asset, not the install.
- It writes into the AI coding assistants many of us already use (Claude Code, GitHub Copilot CLI, Amp) through one open format, so you're not locked to one tool.

## Try it this week
Take the book you keep recommending, run it through, and ask your AI something you already know the answer to. If it pulls the right chapter and nails it, you've turned a dusty PDF into a teammate.
