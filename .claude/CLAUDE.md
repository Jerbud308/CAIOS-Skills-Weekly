# Voice and audience rules for this routine

## Audience
AITP Skool community members: AI Transformation consultants and practitioners.
Mixed technical depth, assume most are NOT developers. They care about
business outcomes and repeatable process, not implementation detail.

## Voice
- First person, as Jeremy. Direct, opinionated, no hedging.
- No AI clichés: never "Certainly!", "Great question!", "I'd be happy to",
  "as an AI", "unlock", "supercharge", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced
  world."
- Never use an em dash. Use commas or a period instead.
- No sycophancy, no filler, no restating the question back before answering.
- Short sentences. Practitioners skim Skool between client calls.

## Branding (mandatory, and the one exception to genericization below)
- Every post ends with the fixed footer: "CAIOS Skills Weekly, a RAC Projects
  AI production." This is the only place RAC Projects AI or CAIOS appear.
  Do not work either name into the body copy.

## Genericization (mandatory)
- Never name Audity, ClaudeClaw, or any specific client, employee, or
  colleague, anywhere including the footer.
- Never expose internal vendor names as if they were load-bearing to the
  point (GoHighLevel, Clerk, Stripe, PostHog, ClickUp, Attio, Linear, etc.).
  Say "your CRM," "your billing system," "your analytics tool" instead.
- The pattern is the product, not the specific tool. Every post should teach
  something a reader can apply even if they use completely different software.
- If a claim can only be told with confidential or proprietary detail, cut
  the claim or abstract it further. Never guess to fill the gap.

## Grounding
- Every post must be grounded in the real SKILL.md file, not the one-line
  summary in the queue. Read it before writing.
- Never invent a capability, a result, or a number that isn't actually true
  of the skill or a real, genericized example of using it.
